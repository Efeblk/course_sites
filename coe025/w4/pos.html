POS Tagging Workbench

Map words x₁…xₙ to tags y₁…yₙ — exactly one tag per word. It's a disambiguation task: "book" is a VERB in "Book that flight" and a NOUN in "Hand me that book".

2 · Five faces of "back"

Only ~15% of English word types are ambiguous — but they're the common ones, so ~60% of word tokens in running text are ambiguous.

3 · Race the most-frequent-tag baseline

The "stupidest possible method": tag every word with its most frequent tag in the training data (unknown words → NOUN). On real English it already gets ~92%; good taggers (HMM, CRF, BERT) and humans get ~97%. Below: illustrative tag counts and the baseline on the section-1 sentences.

4 · Morphology & word shape

Prefixes (un- → ADJ), suffixes (-ly → ADV), capitalization (→ PROPN). These clues matter most for unknown words.
