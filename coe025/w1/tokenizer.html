Tokenizer Lab

A model never sees letters — it sees tokens. Here you build three tokenizers and watch the out-of-vocabulary problem appear and disappear (lecture slides "Terminology", "Vocabulary & Subwords", "BPE", "WordPiece").

1 · Word-level tokens and the OOV problem

The vocabulary is learned from the training text. Every test token not in it becomes [UNK], and the model loses that word.

2 · Train BPE yourself (used by GPT)

Start from characters (_ marks the end of a word). Each step finds the most frequent adjacent pair across the corpus and merges it into one new symbol. Format: word ×count.

3 · WordPiece (used by BERT)

Greedy longest match first: take the longest vocabulary entry that starts the word, then continue with pieces prefixed by ##. If nothing matches, the whole word becomes [UNK]. Edit the vocabulary and see what changes.

WordPiece tokens

The lecture's Turkish example: Yarın gel ##di ##ğin ##de beni bura ##da bula ##ma ##ya ##bilir ##sin ##iz. Agglutinative languages like Turkish are exactly where subwords shine: one stem, many suffixes.
