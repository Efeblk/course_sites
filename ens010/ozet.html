Lecture 2 — Introduction

Exam 1 · L2Nanoscience vs nanotechnology

"If I ask you something in your mid-term exam, the differences between Nanoscience and nanotechnology."

Fark basit: nanoscience = çalışmak, incelemek (teori, kâğıt, bilgisayar); nanotechnology = laboratuvarda yapmak, kontrol etmek (pratik). Enstitüler ikisini de yapıyor, o yüzden adları "Institute for Nanoscience and Nanotechnology".

Write this Nanoscience is the study of phenomena at the nano-size level. It is theoretical: researchers study and prove the properties of materials at this scale (on paper, by computer).
Nanotechnology is the ability to work at the molecular level, atom by atom, to create functional materials, devices and systems, by controlling matter in the 1–100 nm range and using the new properties that appear at that scale. It is practical and experimental (in the lab).
Traps
  • Şıkta creating, controlling, working atom by atom, in the lab → nanotechnology. study, theory → nanoscience.
  • "Nanoscience and nanotechnology are the same thing" → yanlış.

Exam 3 · L3 · most likelyWhat is a nanomaterial?

"Be careful of this description. This is very important. If somebody asks you what is a nanomaterial, you should say a material with at least one dimension in the range 1 to 100 nanometers."

Neden 100? 900 nm, 500 nm'de malzeme hâlâ bulk gibi davranır. 100 nm eşik: oradan aşağı inince surface-to-volume ratio sert biçimde artar ve özellikler değişir.

Write this A nanomaterial is a material with at least one dimension in the nano-size range, 1 to 100 nm. 100 nm is the threshold: below it, the surface area to volume ratio and the share of surface atoms increase drastically, so surface-related properties dominate and the properties of the material change. Above 100 nm (for example 500 or 900 nm) the material still behaves like a bulk material.
Traps
  • "all three dimensions must be 1–100 nm" (bu nanoparticle'ın tanımı, nanomaterial'ın değil)
  • "1 to 1000 nm" (bu matematikteki nano size, bkz. Exam 6)

Lecture 4 — Classification and quantum materials

Exam 4 · L4 · most likelyClassification by dimension (0D, 1D, 2D)

"This slide and the whole information that I will talk in this slide could be a good option for your midterm exam… most probably I will ask you something from this slide."

Başka sınıflandırmalar da var (origin, composition, phase…) ama hoca "bunları ezberlemenizi beklemiyorum" dedi. Önemli olan boyut sınıflandırması. Kural: adındaki sayı, hâlâ büyük kalan (100 nm üstü, "serbest") boyut sayısıdır; nano olan boyut sayısı değil.

Dimensions in 1–100 nmFree (large) dimensionsNameExamples
03bulk material (not nano)—
122D nanomaterialnano-film, nano-plate, nano-coating, nano-layer, sheet (graphene)

Write this Nanomaterials are classified by the number of dimensions that are still outside the nano range. If only one dimension is reduced to 1–100 nm, the material is a 2D nanomaterial (nano-films, coatings, layers, plates). If two dimensions are in the nano range, it is a 1D nanomaterial (nanowires, nanorods, nanofibers, nanotubes). If all three dimensions are in the nano range, there is no free dimension left, so it is a 0D nanomaterial, also called a nanoparticle. If no dimension is below 100 nm, it is a bulk material.
Traps
  • "A 2D nanomaterial has two dimensions in the nano range" (tersi: bir boyutu nano, ikisi büyük)
  • "A nanotube is a 2D nanomaterial" (1D)
  • "A 3D nanomaterial has all three dimensions in the nano range" (öyle bir şey yok; o 0D)

Exam 5 · L4What are nanoparticles?

"If I ask you what are nanoparticles … you should say …" (all three dimensions in nano size, zero-dimensional)
Write this Nanoparticles are zero-dimensional (0D) nanomaterials: all three dimensions are in the nano range (1–100 nm), so there is no free dimension left to decrease.

Exam 6 · L4What is nano size in mathematics?

"Now, I may ask you a question. What is a nano size in mathematics? A nano size in mathematics is a size less than a micron or less than 1000 nanometer."

Dikkat: matematikte 900 nm de "nano"dur, ama nanomaterial sadece 1–100 nm. Fark, özelliklerin 100 nm altında değişmesinden geliyor.

Write this In mathematics, nano size is any size less than one micron (1000 nm): 900, 500, 100, 10 nm are all nano sizes. But a nanomaterial is defined only as 1–100 nm, because properties change below 100 nm. Materials above 100 nm are bulk materials.

Exam 7 · L4 · most likelyAre all nanomaterials quantum materials?

"A good question for your midterm could be, are all nanomaterials quantum materials or not? … All quantum materials could be considered as nanomaterials, but not necessarily all nanomaterials could be considered as quantum materials. This is important to know."

100 nm'deki sert değişimin aynısı ~10 nm civarında bir daha olur; orada quantum physics geçerli. Quantum material = en az bir boyutu 1–10 nm.

one dimension smalltwo dimensions smallthree dimensions small
< 100 nm (nano)nano-film (2D)nanowire (1D)nanoparticle (0D)
< 10 nm (quantum)quantum wellquantum wirequantum dot
Write this No. Quantum materials have at least one dimension in the 1–10 nm range. Since 1–10 nm is inside 1–100 nm, all quantum materials are nanomaterials. But not all nanomaterials are quantum materials: for example, a 50 nm nanomaterial is a nanomaterial but not a quantum material. In the quantum range the forms are called quantum well, quantum wire and quantum dot.

Lecture 5 — Prevention, early diagnosis, radiation

Exam 8 · L5 · written candidateIonizing vs non-ionizing radiation

"So in your exam, if I ask you about ionizing and non-ionizing radiations, you should write these descriptions."

Tek soru: radyasyonun hücreyi iyonize edecek (moleküler bağları kıracak) kadar enerjisi var mı?

Write this Non-ionizing radiation does not have enough energy to ionize our cells, so it is considered safe. Examples: power lines, radio and TV waves, mobile networks, satellite signals, infrared (IR), visible light and low-frequency electromagnetic fields.
Ionizing radiation has enough energy to break the molecular bonds of our cells, so it is harmful. Examples: ultraviolet (UV), X-rays and gamma rays.
Traps
  • "IR / visible light is ionizing"
  • "UV is non-ionizing"

Exam 9 · L5 · written candidateInvasive vs non-invasive techniques

"If I ask you about the invasive techniques and non-invasive techniques, you should say that invasive techniques are techniques that use ionizing radiations such as ultraviolet, UV, X-ray and gamma ray."
Write this Invasive (harmful, non-safe) imaging techniques use ionizing radiation such as UV, X-rays and gamma rays: CT (X-ray), PET and SPECT (gamma rays).
Non-invasive techniques use non-ionizing radiation such as IR, visible light, radio waves and low-frequency electromagnetic fields, or only sound: MRI, ultrasound and optical imaging (optical/fluorescence imaging is called non-invasive or minimally invasive because the dye is injected).

Exam 11 · L6 · written candidateRoles of nanoparticles in MRI

"If I ask you what are the applications of nanoparticles or roles of nanoparticles in MRI? You can consider two points."

Problem: klasik contrast agent gadolinium toksik ve vücutta her yere dağılıyor. Nanoparçacık bu iki sorunu iki yoldan çözüyor: ya ajanın yerine geçiyor, ya da ajanı taşıyor.

Write this 1. Nanoparticles as contrast agents: magnetic nanoparticles such as superparamagnetic iron oxide (SPIO), manganese oxide, rare-earth (gadolinium) oxides and alloys such as FePt can be used as MRI contrast agents to enhance the quality and resolution of the images. They must be biocompatible (not toxic).
2. Nanoparticles as carriers: existing contrast agents such as gadolinium cannot be targeted because their surface cannot be modified. If we encapsulate them inside nanoparticles, we can stabilize them, coat them with a biocompatible polymer and modify the surface to target only the specific site, so they do not spread everywhere in the body. Result: decreased toxicity and side effects.
In both cases the magnetic properties of the agent must be kept after modification.

Exam 12 · L6 · written candidatePhotoacoustic imaging (PAI): procedure, advantages, contrast agents

"If I ask you what is the procedure of photoacoustic, you should completely describe these three steps. If I ask you about the advantages, here it is. And if I ask you about the contrast agents, these are the contrast agents… Be careful, this photoacoustic is different from ultrasonic."

Mantık: ışık girer, ses çıkar. Lazer dokuyu ısıtır, ısınan doku genleşir, genleşme ses dalgası üretir.

Write this Procedure (3 steps):
1. The tissue is illuminated with a laser pulse.
2. The absorbed light excites the electrons; the excited electrons relax back to the ground state and the absorbed energy is released as heat.
3. The heated area tries to expand; this abrupt thermal expansion produces an outgoing thermoacoustic (sound) wave, which is detected by an ultrasound transducer and turned into an image.
Advantages: it gives anatomical, functional and molecular contrast in a single modality, and it can image several centimeters deep.
Contrast agents: plasmonic nanoparticles such as gold nanoparticles, silver nanoparticles and single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWNT), because they have a superior ability to absorb light and an optimized light-to-acoustic conversion efficiency.
Traps
  • "Photoacoustic imaging is the same as ultrasound" (PAI'de giriş laser/light, US'de giriş de çıkış da sound)
  • "The signal detected in PAI is light" (algılanan şey sound wave)

Exam 13 · L6 · written candidateHow do micro/nano-bubbles work in ultrasound?

"I have written in this specifically because if I ask you, you know how to respond me."

Ses derine gittikçe echo zayıflar, o yüzden contrast agent gerekir. Bubbles şu an klinikte kullanılan tek ultrasound contrast agent; büyüklükleri yüzünden damar içinde (intravascular) kalırlar. Patlamazlar, şişip büzülürler.

Write this Micro/nano-bubbles (a gas core with a shell of protein, lipid, polymer or a hybrid) are the only clinically used ultrasound contrast agents. When they are exposed to ultrasound waves, they undergo volumetric oscillation: they expand and contract, without bursting. This produces a very strong nonlinear acoustic response and high backscatter, which enhances the echo and increases the signal-to-noise ratio, giving better contrast and better images. The first bubbles had no shell and burst easily; later shells (protein, lipid, polymer, hybrid) made them stable. Polymer shells give the most control.
Traps
  • "Bubbles enhance the image by bursting"
  • "Ultrasound is invasive"

Lecture 7 — Biosensors (en önemli ders)

Son dersin sonunda bir öğrenci "en çok neye odaklanalım?" diye sordu. Cevap: "Today, as I told you, the transducers are important. The performance slide is very important. These are the parts that are mostly examinable."

Exam 14 · L7Biosensor, receptor (biocatalyst), analyte, transducer

"If I ask you, what is a biocatalyst or receptor, you should name these ones. If I ask you that if a thermal transducer is considered as a receptor, of course not… If I ask you in your midterm next week if the antigens could be considered as receptor. Of course they are not. Antigens are analytes."

Zincir: analyte → receptor (biocatalyst) → transducer → signal output. Analyte = aradığımız şey (hastalığın işareti, örn. antigen). Receptor = analyte'e yapışan parça. Transducer = olan değişimi okunabilir sinyale çeviren parça.

Write this A biosensor is a measuring (sensing) device that detects an analyte, a part or sign of the cause of a disease, to diagnose the disease. It has two main parts. The receptor (biocatalyst) is in contact with the analyte and binds it with a lock-and-key interaction; it can be an enzyme, an antibody, a nucleic acid or a cell. The transducer converts the biological signal of this interaction (a change in current, potential, heat, mass or light) into a quantifiable, readable signal. Types of transducers: electrochemical, optical, thermal and piezoelectric.
Traps
  • "An antigen is a receptor" (antigen = analyte; antibody = receptor)
  • "A thermal transducer is a receptor"
  • "Which is NOT a biocatalyst?" → enzyme, antibody, nucleic acid, cell dışındaki şık (örn. antigen, transducer).

Exam 15 · L7 · most likelyAmperometric, calorimetric and optical biosensors

"I expect you, specifically for your midterm, to memorize these three different transducers… I may ask you to write the definition of each of them, or … in a multiple choice question, which of them is correct… Most probably, I will ask you in the mid-term."

Biosensor adını transducer'ından alır. Ölçülen şey: amperometric = akım, calorimetric = ısı, optical = ışık.

Write this An amperometric biosensor has an amperometric transducer that measures the current produced in proportion to the bioreaction between the receptor and the analyte (more analyte → more bioreaction → more current).
A calorimetric (thermal) biosensor measures the amount of heat produced or absorbed by the bioreaction (a change in temperature).
An optical biosensor measures the change in light intensity (light produced or absorbed) when the specific bioreaction happens.

Tablonun geri kalanı (MC'de çıkabilir): potentiometric = potential, impedance; thermistor = temperature; piezo-electric = mass (vibrations); acoustic = amplitude, phase, frequency; fluorescence = intensity, colour.

Traps
  • "An amperometric biosensor measures the amount of heat produced" (hocanın kendi örneği; akım ölçer)
  • "A calorimetric biosensor measures current / light"
  • "A potentiometric biosensor measures current" (potential ölçer)

Exam 16 · L7 · most likelyPerformance parameters of a biosensor

"For your midterm, be careful of this slide also. This is important. I may ask you to describe these parameters, or I may ask you, for example, if the selectivity is how sharply the signal change. Of course it is not. This is the definition for sensitivity. I may tell you that the limit of detection is the maximum concentration. Of course it is not. It is minimum concentration."

Bu parametreler bir biosensor'ün fiyatını belirler (bir glukoz sensörü neden diğerinden pahalı).

Write this Selectivity: the ability to detect the target analyte in the presence of other interfering species (similar molecules).
Sensitivity: how sharply the signal changes with variations in the analyte concentration.
Response time (speed): how long it takes for the sensor to stabilize at a reliable value.
Limit of detection: the minimum concentration of analyte the biosensor can detect. Lower is better, because detecting low concentrations allows early diagnosis.
Accuracy (precision): the degree of reproducibility and reliability (the same sample gives the same signal every time).
Durability (stability): the ability to maintain the signal over time, which is important for in vivo (implanted) sensors.
Cost and ease of use: everyone should be able to buy it and use it easily at home.
Traps
  • "Selectivity is how sharply the signal changes with concentration" (bu sensitivity)
  • "Limit of detection is the maximum concentration" (minimum)
  • "Accuracy is the ability to maintain the signal over time" (bu durability)
  • "Response time is the minimum concentration…" (karışık tanım şıkları: her tanımı doğru isme eşle)

L3: neden boyut önemli

  • Surface area to volume ratio boyut küçülünce artar; nano'da yüzey atomları içtekileri geçer.
  • Catalytic activity (chemical): boyut küçülünce activation energy düşer, activity artar. Aynı catalyst'i nano boyutta kullanmak yeni catalyst icat etmekten ucuz. Kullanım: petroleum, energy conversion, pollutant removal.
  • Melting point düşer: surface atoms daha az komşuya bağlı, ısıya ilk onlar maruz kalır ve ilk onlar erir; nano'da çoğunluk yüzey.
  • Electrical: normal tel incelince direnci artar ve ısınır; carbon nanotube küçük çapta bile iletir, bu yüzden çipler küçülebildi.
  • Biological: hücre zarı ve blood-brain barrier'dan kolay geçer, biocompatibility yüksek. Hoca: sağlık uygulamaları "the most important".

Son kontrol: cevabı söyle, sonra aç

Cevabı İngilizce, sesli ya da kâğıda söyle; sonra kontrol et.

Difference between nanoscience and nanotechnology?

Nanoscience = the study at the nano-size level (theory). Nanotechnology = working atom by atom to create and control materials, devices and systems at 1–100 nm (practice, lab).

Which is a physical property: reactivity, conductivity, melting point, strength?

Melting point. Reactivity = chemical, conductivity = electrical, strength = mechanical.

Define a nanomaterial.

A material with at least one dimension in the range 1–100 nm.

A nano-film is 1D, 2D or 0D?

2D (one dimension nano, two dimensions large). Nanowire/nanotube = 1D, nanoparticle = 0D.

What are nanoparticles?

0D nanomaterials: all three dimensions in 1–100 nm.

Nano size in mathematics?

Anything below 1 µm (1000 nm).

Are all nanomaterials quantum materials?

No. All quantum materials (1–10 nm) are nanomaterials, but a 50 nm nanomaterial is not quantum.

Ionizing vs non-ionizing? Examples?

Ionizing has enough energy to break molecular bonds of cells (UV, X-ray, gamma). Non-ionizing does not (radio, TV, mobile, IR, visible light, low-frequency EM fields).

Name invasive and non-invasive imaging techniques.

Invasive: CT, PET, SPECT. Non-invasive: MRI, ultrasound, optical imaging.

Two roles of nanoparticles in MRI?

1) Biocompatible magnetic NPs (e.g. SPIO) as contrast agents. 2) NPs as carriers: encapsulate, stabilize, coat and target existing agents like gadolinium → less toxicity and side effects. Keep the magnetic properties.

Three steps of photoacoustic imaging?

Laser pulse illuminates tissue → electrons excited, relax, energy released as heat → thermal expansion makes a sound wave detected by an ultrasound transducer.

PAI advantages and contrast agents?

Anatomical + functional + molecular contrast in one modality; several cm depth. Au NPs, Ag NPs, SWNT (strong light absorption, good light-to-acoustic conversion).

How do bubbles enhance ultrasound?

Volumetric oscillation (expand and contract, no bursting) → strong nonlinear acoustic response → higher signal-to-noise ratio.

Name the biocatalysts/receptors. Is an antigen a receptor?

Enzyme, antibody, nucleic acid, cell. No, the antigen is the analyte.

Amperometric / calorimetric / optical biosensor measures what?

Current in proportion to the bioreaction / heat produced or absorbed / change in light intensity.

Selectivity vs sensitivity? Limit of detection?

Selectivity = detecting the target among interfering species. Sensitivity = how sharply the signal changes with concentration. LOD = the minimum detectable concentration.

Accuracy vs durability?

Accuracy = reproducibility and reliability. Durability = maintaining the signal over time.
