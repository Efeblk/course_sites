Five short videos on Week 4

Each one explains a single idea from AI in clinical decision-making as a small animated story, in about a minute and a half. They are in English, like the exam.

01Thresholds 02Confusion matrix 03Prevalence & accuracy 04Prediction to action 05Augmented intelligence

Thresholds and alert fatigue

1:13

A model scores every patient on a ward. Lowering the alert threshold from 10% to 5% catches 14 more real cases but adds about 430 false alarms, until the one alert that mattered gets swiped away. There is no universal threshold.

The confusion matrix

1:46

200 patients sort themselves into true and false positives and negatives. Sensitivity and specificity read down the columns (the truth); PPV and NPV read across the rows (what the model said).

Prevalence, PPV and the 99% accuracy trap

1:31

The same model on three populations: PPV falls from 90% to 50% to 8% as the condition gets rarer. Then a model that says “nobody is sick” is 99% accurate and finds no one.

From prediction to action

1:31

The lecturer's story of an alert that nobody owns, then the six steps: define the target, use only data available at prediction time, validate, choose a threshold, link it to a response, measure the whole process.

Augmented intelligence and trust

1:30

The navigation analogy, automation versus augmentation, automation bias, commission and omission errors, under-trust, calibrated trust, and what makes human oversight meaningful.
