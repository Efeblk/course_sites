1a. Systems Analyst'in rolü

Discussion · Ch1 s30–31
Explain the role of a Systems Analyst, detailing what they study and the required understanding they must possess to perform their job effectively.

Tanımı yaz, sonra ne inceler (problems, opportunities, directives, needs) ve neyi bilmeli (organizational model, techniques and tools, team work). Üç kelimeyi de birer cümleyle açıkla.

  • A systems analyst is a specialist who studies the problems, opportunities, directives and needs of an organization to decide how people, data, processes and IT can best improve the business.
  • Problems: real or expected situations that need corrective action. Opportunities: chances to improve even without complaints. Directives: (legal) orders to change, whether or not anyone complained.
  • Must understand the company's organizational model: who is responsible for which processes and decisions, and what information is required by whom.
  • Must know a variety of techniques and tools to plan, design and implement information systems, and work in a team.

1b. Verification ve Validation

Discussion · Ch1 s41–45
Discuss the relationship between Verification and Validation in ensuring Software Quality, including the core question each activity seeks to answer.

İkisini ayrı tanımla, çekirdek sorularını tırnakla yaz, birer etkinlik örneği ver. "İlişki" kısmı: ikisi birlikte software quality assurance'ı destekler ve önce verification, sonra validation.

  • Software quality = the degree to which software satisfies its requirements; quality assurance is supported by verification and validation.
  • Verification: checks that the product is built according to the requirements and specifications (does the code implement the design?). Question: "Are we building the product right?" Activities: inspections and reviews.
  • Validation: checks that the finished system meets the specified user requirements. Question: "Are we building the right product?" Activity: software testing.
  • Relationship: first verification, then validation; together they remove defects, and defects found earlier cost less to repair.

4a. Fishbone: amacı, çizimi, parçaları

10 puan · Ch5 s26–28
Define the purpose of the Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram within the context of SAD. Describe the process required to construct it, detailing the function and placement of its main components.
  • Purpose: a cause-and-effect tool for a quality issue that has arisen or may arise; it shows all possible causes visually to find the root cause. In SAD it is the problem discovery tool of the requirements analysis phase (to identify, analyze and solve problems).
  • Process: (1) draw the head on the right and write the problem (effect) in it; (2) draw the backbone to the left; (3) draw branches for 4–8 main causes; (4) attach causes and sub-causes to each main cause, usually from a brainstorming session.
  • Components: head = effect/problem (right) · spine/backbone = leads to the effect · main bones = cause categories · small bones = sub-causes.

4b. Fishbone senaryosu: PSRAS

10 puan
PSRAS (Project Scheduling and Resource Allocation System): after one month in production, High Instability and Unreliable Performance: frequent crashes and very slow response at peak hours. 1) Define the effect. 2) 4–8 main cause categories from the IS components and SAD activities. 3) Draw it. 4) At least two specific sub-causes under each category.

Ana kemikleri ezberleme: soru sana söylüyor. IS'in 5 bileşeni (hardware, software, data, process, people; Ch1 s17) + SAD etkinlikleri (requirements, design, testing, deployment; Ch2). Alt nedenleri slayt kavramlarından seç: performance / reliability NFR, testing levels, training, data conversion.

Effect (head): PSRAS: high instability and unreliable performance (frequent crashes, slow response at peak hours).

  • Hardware: server capacity too small for peak usage; no load balancing or backup server.
  • Software: memory leaks and unhandled exceptions cause crashes; tightly coupled, one failure stops all.
  • Data: large tables without indexes slow queries; errors from data entry/conversion at deployment.
  • People: users not trained on the new system; inexperienced builders / testers.
  • Requirements: no measurable performance NFR (e.g. response time at peak load) and no reliability / availability NFR (e.g. mean time to failure).
  • Design: architecture not designed for the peak workload; weak database design.
  • Testing: no system or load testing under peak conditions; no regression testing after changes; acceptance testing rushed.
  • Process / deployment: rushed go-live without beta testing; no post-deployment review.

Sınavda 4–8 ana kemik yeter; her birinde en az 2 alt neden. Kendi alt nedenlerini yazabilirsin, yeter ki somut ve konuya bağlı olsun.

1b. Functional ve Non-functional

Discussion · Ch5 s9–25
How do functional and non-functional requirements differ? Provide examples.
  • Functional: activities and services the application must provide; how the product must behave. e.g. "The student shall display his/her weekly schedule." "The mailing service shall send a welcome email when a visitor creates an account."
  • Non-functional: general characteristics that define a satisfactory system: quality attributes, constraints, interfaces, error handling. e.g. performance: "6 s or less response for 5,000 users per hour"; security: "password at least 8 characters with one upper-case letter and one digit"; availability: "98% of business hours"; constraint: "implemented in Java".
  • Short test: what the system does → functional; how well / under which limits → non-functional.

2. Fill-in-the-blank ve True/False

20 puan
a. In Agile, a short development cycle aimed at delivering small, incremental improvements is known as a ______.
b. The concept of ______ refers to the degree to which software components depend on each other. Lower dependency typically results in better maintainability.
T/F a. A class diagram in UML is used to represent the dynamic behavior of a system.
T/F b. Lines of Code (LOC) estimation is independent of the programming language used.
  1. sprint (Scrum, Ch3 s33–34)
  2. coupling. Not in the chapter 1–7 slides; it belongs to design concepts. Remember the pair: low coupling = parts depend little on each other = easier to maintain.
  3. False. The class diagram is a structural diagram (classes, attributes, operations, relationships). Behavior is shown by behavioral diagrams such as sequence, activity or state machine (Ch6 s58, Ch7 s51).
  4. False. LOC depends on the language: the same algorithm needs a different number of lines in different languages. Function points are the language-independent measure (Ch3 s50, Ch4 s4).
